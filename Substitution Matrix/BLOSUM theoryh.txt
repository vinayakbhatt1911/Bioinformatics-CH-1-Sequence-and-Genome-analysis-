BLOSUM (BLOcks SUbstitution Matrix)

BLOSUM stands for BLOcks SUbstitution Matrix. BLOSUM matrices are substitution matrices used to score amino-acid substitutions during protein sequence alignment.

Basic Idea

BLOSUM matrices are constructed from conserved regions, or blocks, of protein sequences. These blocks are regions where gaps are generally not allowed and where proteins show meaningful sequence conservation.

The observed amino-acid substitutions within these blocks are used to calculate scores for different amino-acid pairs.

Common BLOSUM Matrices
Matrix	General use
BLOSUM45	More divergent protein sequences
BLOSUM62	General-purpose protein alignment
BLOSUM80	More closely related protein sequences

The important rule is:

Higher BLOSUM number → generally more closely related sequences.

So:

BLOSUM80 → closer sequences
BLOSUM62 → general purpose
BLOSUM45 → more divergent sequences

This is the opposite direction from PAM numbering.

How BLOSUM Scores Substitutions

A BLOSUM matrix contains amino acids on both axes:

       A   R   N   D
A      4  -1  -2  -2
R     -1   5   0  -2
N     -2   0   6   1
D     -2  -2   1   6

The actual values depend on which BLOSUM matrix is being used.

For example, a positive value means that the particular amino-acid pairing is relatively favorable, while a negative value represents an unfavorable substitution.

These scores are used by alignment algorithms to calculate the diagonal score when aligning protein sequences.

How BLOSUM Is Different from Simple Match/Mismatch

Instead of treating every different amino acid as identical:

Match     = +1
Mismatch  = -1

BLOSUM gives different scores to different amino-acid substitutions based on their observed evolutionary behavior.

Therefore, a substitution between two chemically or evolutionarily similar amino acids can receive a better score than a highly unlikely substitution.

BLOSUM62

BLOSUM62 is one of the most commonly used substitution matrices for general protein sequence alignment.

It provides a useful balance between closely related and moderately divergent protein sequences and is widely used in sequence similarity searches and alignment tools.

Applications

BLOSUM matrices are used in:

Protein sequence alignment
Local and global alignment
Homology detection
Similarity searches
BLAST
EMBOSS Needle
EMBOSS Water
Evolutionary analysis
PAM vs BLOSUM: Key Difference

The main conceptual difference is their construction:

PAM
→ Based on an evolutionary model of accepted amino-acid mutations.

BLOSUM
→ Derived from observed substitutions in conserved protein blocks.

Key takeaway

BLOSUM matrices provide biologically informed substitution scores derived from conserved protein sequence blocks, with lower-numbered matrices generally suited to more divergent sequences and higher-numbered matrices to more closely related sequences.
